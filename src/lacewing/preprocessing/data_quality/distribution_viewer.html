Layers A/B statistics (dyn_range, net_slope, trace_min): threshold is computed once from the chip's NTC well and applied to every well. Layer D statistic: threshold is computed per well from each well's own disagreement distribution.
Why this matters. All rules here operate on the per-well distribution of one of these statistics. The percentile rule is robust to any distribution shape, but uses pure rank — no notion of "how stretched is the population". The symmetric MAD rule estimates spread, but assumes the distribution is approximately symmetric. When the data is heavily skewed (e.g. trace_min or Layer D's L2 distance, which are bounded on one side), symmetric MAD systematically misses the long tail. The one-sided MAD rules use only the half-distribution on the side of the threshold, so they ignore the opposite tail entirely. The IQR (Tukey) rule is the classical skew-aware alternative: Q1 − k·IQR (lower) / Q3 + k·IQR (upper). Because Q1 and Q3 are independently anchored on each side of the median, the upper and lower fences automatically widen with the tail on their own side, with no symmetry assumption. k=1.5 is the textbook "outlier" cutoff (Tukey 1977); k=3.0 is the stricter "far-out" cutoff.
